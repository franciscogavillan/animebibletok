# 00 — PRODUCTION CHARTER

**Project:** GENESIS — a cinematic KJV anime series
**Format:** ~148 vertical episodes (9:16), 45–60 s each, TikTok-first
**Source text:** King James Version (Authorized Version, standard 1769 Oxford text as printed in modern KJV Bibles)

This document holds the rules that override everything else. The other Bible
documents give the details, and every episode is checked against this one.

---

## 1. The Golden Rule

> The audience should forget the footage was generated by AI. They should think: *"I'm watching an anime."*

When two goals conflict, the priority order is fixed:

| Choose | over |
|---|---|
| Biblical fidelity | viral gimmicks |
| Continuity | spectacle |
| Better acting | more visual effects |
| A believable shot | a flashy shot |
| Storytelling | effects |
| Quality | speed |

## 2. Scriptural fidelity rules

### 2.1 Text

- The **KJV is the only source text** for narration, God's speech, character dialogue and quotations. Do not use NKJV, NIV, ESV, NLT, NASB, paraphrases or "modernised KJV".
- Keep KJV spelling and forms exactly: *subtil*, *thee/thou*, *an help meet*, *Cherubims*, *LORD* (small caps in subtitles), *shew*, and so on.
- Every scene carries its **book:chapter:verse** reference.

### 2.2 Labels (mandatory in every script)

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `NARRATOR — KJV` | Narrator speaking KJV text verbatim |
| `GOD — KJV` | God's speech, KJV verbatim |
| `<CHARACTER> — KJV` | A character's speech, KJV verbatim |
| `ADAPTATION — NOT KJV` | Any bridging words not found in Scripture. Use rarely, never in God's mouth, and never presented as Scripture. |
| `ON-SCREEN TEXT — KJV` | Verse text shown on screen rather than read aloud |

**Default: zero ADAPTATION lines.** Silence and visual storytelling come before invented dialogue.

### 2.3 Voiceover abridgment policy

Genesis is dense. A calm KJV read runs about **2.4–2.6 words per second**, so a 60-second episode holds about **120–150 spoken words** and still leaves room to breathe. When a passage is longer:

1. **Split the episode first** (Phase 4 rule). Suffix letters keep the master numbering intact, e.g. `09A`, `09B`.
2. **Abridge only when splitting would produce an empty episode.** An abridgment must follow all of these:
   - Omit only **whole verses** or **enumerative clauses** (lists such as "and over the fowl of the air…").
   - Mark every omission with `…` in the script.
   - **Never** paraphrase, reorder, merge or substitute words.
   - **Never** omit any part of a **command, promise, blessing, curse, covenant or judgment** spoken by God. These are always read complete.
   - Every episode file includes the **full KJV text** of its passage, with omitted portions marked, so the full text can be used in captions or long-form cuts.

### 2.4 Events

- Do not change who did what, the chronology, relationships, deaths, covenants, promises, genealogies, specified locations, God's commands or major dialogue.
- Visual expansion is allowed only where Scripture is brief (landscapes, livestock, weather, travel, labour). It must never contradict or add to the plot.
- **Where Scripture is silent on a visual detail** (the fruit, the mark of Cain, the cherubim's form), the design is labelled `PRODUCTION CHOICE` in the relevant Bible so it is never mistaken for a scriptural claim.
- **No false cliffhangers.** An episode may end on a moment of real tension in the text, but never on invented peril.

### 2.5 God's representation

- God is **never** shown as an ordinary human character or an anime protagonist.
- God's presence is conveyed through **voice, light, wind, cloud, fire and the environment's reaction** (trees bending, water stilling, animals turning).
- **Divine light is rare by design.** Ordinary scenes are lit by believable sources, so divine phenomena look exceptional.
- Where Scripture describes the LORD appearing *as a man* (Gen 18, the three visitors; Gen 32, the wrestler), the figure is a man in appearance. Faces are kept unresolved through backlight, night, veiling or framing, never through glow.
- God's voice does not come from a visible mouth.

## 3. The Quality Directive (non-negotiable)

This project must not look like generic AI content. These rules apply to every shot.

### 3.1 Banned aesthetics

Do not use any of the following:

- generic AI Bible imagery, slideshow compositions or stock "AI cinematic" frames
- static talking characters, or characters posing instead of acting
- characters staring into the lens without a story reason
- repetitive close-ups, excessive slow motion, constant dramatic zooms, unmotivated camera moves
- random particles, excessive glow, artificial lens flares, fake volumetric fog, excessive depth-of-field blur
- plastic or waxy skin, perfectly symmetrical faces, over-smooth animation
- floating objects, morphing anatomy, sliding feet, rubbery limbs, weightless running
- faces that change between shots, clothing that changes between shots
- random environmental detail, generic "epic fantasy" imagery
- effects added only because they look impressive

**If an effect does not serve the story, remove it.**

### 3.2 Every shot must justify itself

Each shot in an episode states a **Purpose**: why this camera position, lens, composition, movement, light and subject placement. A shot whose purpose is "looks cool" gets cut.

### 3.3 Acting over posing

Characters are film actors, not mannequins. Every character shot specifies:

- eye direction and what they are looking at
- intentional expression, preferring subtle over exaggerated
- body language, breathing and weight shifts
- hand interaction with objects, ground or other people
- reactions to what is happening around them

Dialogue is never a pasted-on voiceover over a still face. Speakers have a reason to look where they look, they move, they pause, and listeners react.

### 3.4 Grounded world

- Environments are inhabited, with insects, animals, footprints, wind in leaves, water moving, smoke and dust.
- Settlements have mud-brick, stone, woven cloth, pottery, livestock, fire, tools and field work.
- Human variation: asymmetry, skin texture, imperfect hair, age-appropriate wrinkles, dust, sweat, travel wear, and clothing that shows use.

### 3.5 Physics

- Feet plant and push off the ground. Hands actually contact what they touch.
- Animals move according to their anatomy.
- Wind moves hair, cloth, vegetation, dust and smoke in the **same direction** within a shot.
- Rain wets hair, darkens fabric and makes surfaces reflective.

### 3.6 The shot test (run on every shot before generating)

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| STORY | Does this shot communicate something? |
| CHARACTER | Is the character actually acting? |
| CAMERA | Does the camera position serve the moment? |
| ENVIRONMENT | Does the world feel physically real? |
| CONTINUITY | Does it match the previous shots and the Bible? |
| PHYSICS | Does the movement make physical sense? |
| QUALITY | Would it fit in a professional animated series? |
| **AI-SLOP** | **Would a viewer instantly recognise this as generic AI footage? If yes, redesign the shot.** |

## 4. Production workflow

| Phase | Output | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Master Bible | `bible/00`–`05` | Visual language and character designs locked |
| 2 — Episodes | Batches of 5–10 in `episodes/` | Follow the episode template |
| 3 — Review | `reviews/batch-NN-review.md` | KJV accuracy, chronology, continuity, theology, pacing, runtime, slop test |
| 4 — Refinement | Split dense episodes, add visual storytelling to thin ones | Update the roadmap and the continuity ledger |

**Before writing or generating any episode:** re-read the Character Bible entries for everyone in it, the previous episode's continuity notes, and the Continuity Ledger (`bible/05`).
