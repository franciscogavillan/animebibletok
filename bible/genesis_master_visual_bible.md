# GENESIS — MASTER VISUAL BIBLE
**Cinematic KJV Anime Series · Version 1.3 · TikTok @AnimeBibleTok**
Scope: series-wide rules, plus the design lock for **Arc I (Creation & Eden, Episodes 01–10C, Genesis 1:1–3:24)**. Characters and environments for later arcs are added to this document *before* their arc goes into production, using the same format.

**Order of authority:** (1) the KJV text, (2) the Non-Negotiable Visual Quality Directive (`bible/quality_directive.md`), (3) this Bible, (4) individual episode packages. If a shot conflicts with this Bible, either the shot changes or the Bible is formally amended (bump the version number and log the change in §14).

---

## 1. SERIES IDENTITY

**Logline:** The Book of Genesis, told word-for-word from the King James Version, as a prestige anime series.

**Look statement:** *A hand-drawn prestige TV anime look applied to a grounded ancient Near East.* The stylization is in the character design, the acting and the composition. The world itself (its light, water, dust, cloth, stone and animals) behaves physically.

**Three pillars**
1. **Grounded.** Ordinary scenes are physically believable, so the divine moments feel like exceptions.
2. **Restrained.** Every effect, camera move and music cue has to justify its presence. Silence is a tool.
3. **Authored.** Every shot looks chosen by a cinematographer, not generated by a prompt.

## 1.2 FORMAT & EPISODE NUMBERING (v1.3)
- **Runtime: 61–90 seconds per episode.** The 61-second minimum is a hard floor (TikTok monetization requires videos over one minute). Aim for 64–88 seconds so recorded narration can drift without breaking either limit.
- **Lettered episodes.** When the KJV text for a roadmap episode cannot fit in 90 seconds without cutting divine speech, the episode splits into lettered parts (`02A`, `02B`, …). The master roadmap numbering (1–148) never changes; a letter only subdivides it. See `episodes/ROADMAP.md`.
- **Arc I is 18 episodes:** 01, 02A, 02B, 03A, 03B, 04A, 04B, 05, 06A, 06B, 07A, 07B, 08, 09A, 09B, 10A, 10B, 10C.

## 2. RENDERING STYLE

**Characters**
- Realistic proportions (about 7–7.5 heads tall). Eyes are slightly stylized, not oversized. No chibi.
- Clean line art with slight variation in line weight. Lines are heavier on silhouettes and lighter on interior detail.
- Two- or three-tone cel shading. A soft gradient on skin only where the key light falls. No airbrushed "plastic" skin.
- Skin texture is suggested in close-ups: uneven tone, sun damage, freckling, creases and pores, all drawn rather than photoreal.

**Backgrounds**
- Painterly and hand-painted, with visible brush texture in skies, water, rock and vegetation.
- Backgrounds carry more detail than characters, as in standard anime practice. This is what sells "real studio."

**Finishing (applied in edit)**
- Subtle film grain on every shot, at a constant strength across the series.
- Mild halation only around genuinely bright sources (fire, divine light). No chromatic aberration, no lens flares, no light leaks.
- **Motion cadence test:** AI video at a smooth 24 fps reads as "AI." For character-acting shots, test conforming character motion to a held-frame cadence (an "on twos" feel, roughly 12 fps) while keeping camera moves at 24. Adopt it series-wide if it tests well; don't mix the two approaches within one arc.

## 4. LIGHTING RULES

1. **Motivated sources only:** sun, moon, stars, fire, torches, oil lamps, lightning, sky bounce and reflection off water or sand.
2. **No default rim light, no default golden glow, no god-rays** unless the source physically produces them (for example, sun through dust in a tent opening).
3. **The divine exception.** Divine light is recognized by its *quality*, never by flare or spectacle:
   - It has no visible source object and no lens flare.
   - It is even and sourceless, filling space rather than beaming into it.
   - The world reacts to it: water, dust, animals and people respond physically.
4. **Before Day 4 (Genesis 1:14–19) there is no sun, moon or stars on screen.** The light of Days 1–3 is sourceless. This is a fidelity rule, not a style choice.
5. Night scenes stay dark. Use moonlight and firelight honestly and let shadows go black.

## 5. CAMERA LANGUAGE (VERTICAL 9:16)

**Lens equivalents**

| Use | Focal Length | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Establishing / scale | 18–24mm | Geography, vastness, isolation |
| Movement / medium | 35mm | Walking, working, interaction |
| Close-ups | 50–85mm | Emotion, reaction |
| Extreme close-up | 85–100mm | Rare: major moments only |

**Vertical composition rules**
- Use the height of the frame: sky against ground, above against below, a figure small beneath something vast.
- Stack depth vertically: foreground at the bottom, subject in the middle, world at the top.
- Put faces in the upper third.
- **Safe zones:** keep essential action and text out of the bottom ~20% of the frame (TikTok captions and UI) and the right-hand ~15% (buttons).

**Movement vocabulary**

| Move | Meaning in this series |
|---|---|
| Locked-off | Tension, stillness, reverence, waiting |
| Slow push-in | Realization; something is about to matter |
| Pull-back / crane up | Scale, consequence, isolation |
| Tracking | Journey; following purpose or movement |
| Tilt up / down | Relationship between heaven and earth; revealing height |
| Handheld (subtle) | Chaos and violence only (Cain, Sodom, the pit) |
| Dutch angle | Reserved. Moral disorder only, used at most a few times in the entire series |

**Hard rules:** one camera move per shot at most. No zooms (use dolly moves instead). No moves that exist only "to add energy." Shots run 3–8 seconds, which suits clip-generation lengths and TikTok pacing, for roughly 8–12 shots per episode.

## 6. DIVINE & SUPERNATURAL REPRESENTATION

### GOD
- **Never** shown in human form, as a face, as a figure inside light, or as a hand reaching from the clouds.
- Represented by **voice** and by **the world's response**: water shivers, wind moves, light arrives, animals turn their heads, people fall still.
- "The voice of the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of the day" (3:8) is shown as wind moving through the garden, leaves and grass turning in a passing line, birdsong stopping, and Adam and Eve reacting. No figure.
- The forming of man from dust (2:7) is shown as ground-level dust drawing together in close-up, then breath: wind enters and the chest rises for the first time. No divine hands.

### TWO TELLINGS: GENESIS 1 VS GENESIS 2 (v1.3)
Genesis 1 gives the whole creation from above; Genesis 2:4 onward retells the making of man from the ground. The camera makes the shift readable without adding words:
- **Genesis 1 (Ep 01–05) is shot at God's-eye scale:** high, wide and distant, with the world as the actor. People appear in Ep 04A–04B only as **distant figures, faces withheld**.
- **Genesis 2–3 (Ep 06A onward) is shot at human scale:** ground level, 35–85mm, close to skin, soil and water. Faces are revealed here: **Adam in 06A (2:7), Eve in 07B (2:22).**
- Ep 06A opens by descending from the high Genesis 1 vantage down to a single patch of bare ground. That shot marks the change of telling.

### DAY ENDINGS: "THE EVENING AND THE MORNING" (v1.3)
Every creation day ends with the soft day/night boundary passing over the world (established Ep 01, S08), never a sunset before Day 4. **Direction is locked:** darkness sweeps right to left; light returns from the left. Vary the camera angle each day so the device never repeats as the same shot. From Day 4 on, the sun exists and evening and morning may be shown with the real sun, still keeping that direction. **Day 7 has no evening and morning in the text, so Ep 05 ends without the sweep.**

### THE SPIRIT OF GOD (Genesis 1:2)
- **Visual signature, "the flattened path":** a vast moving front passes over the water. Ahead of it the surface is rough and dark; behind it, the water lies glassy and smoothed, with a fine veil of spray lifting and settling.
- No figure, no dove, no glow.
- **Series callback:** reuse this signature in Genesis 8:1 ("God made a wind to pass over the earth, and the waters asswaged").

## 7. CHARACTER BIBLE

### 7.1 Human design system (all characters, all arcs)
- **Ethnicity and region:** ancient Near Eastern and Semitic peoples by default, with Egyptians visually distinct in Arc IX onward. Skin tones range from olive to deep brown. Hair is black to dark brown with natural textures. **No default pale anime skin and no light-colored anime hair.**
- **Individuality:** every recurring character gets at least three permanent distinguishing features (asymmetries, marks, proportions, habits) so they read at a glance and hold across generations.
- **No "anime-pretty" template faces.** Vary jaw, nose, brow, eye spacing, mouth width, ears and neck.
- **Aging:** long-lived patriarchs are shown by life stage relative to their stated lifespan, not by literal years. Every character who spans more than one arc gets an aging sheet with two to four age states before their first appearance.
- **Wear:** people who travel look traveled (dust, sun, frayed hems). People who work look like it (calluses, stains, posture).

### 7.2 Nudity & framing rule (Genesis 2:25; 3:1–7)
Adam and Eve are unclothed before the Fall. Fidelity is maintained through **staging, not exposure**:
- Close-ups and medium close-ups are framed at or above the collarbones.
- Wider shots use foliage, water, distance, Eve's hair, over-the-shoulder angles from behind at the upper back, or silhouette.
- **No generated frame contains nudity.** Every prompt states the framing explicitly (for example, "framed from the collarbones up").
- This also keeps the series within platform rules and away from generation-filter failures.

### CH-SERPENT (Genesis 3:1–15)
**Appears:** 07B (end-of-episode glimpse only, no dialogue), 08, 09A (in the tree, watching), 09B (in the tree, watching), 10A (the curse)
- **Scale:** about 4–5 m long. The upper body is roughly as thick as a man's forearm.
- **Surface:** fine, tight scales in deep olive to bronze, with a pattern of dark interlocking diamonds along the back and a pale cream underside. A faint iridescence appears **only** when direct light strikes it.
- **Head:** narrow and elegant, not a monstrous viper. **Eyes:** amber with vertical pupils. **No glow**; they reflect light only as real animal eyes do.
- **Posture before the curse:** poised among the tree's branches, with the upper third of the body lifted and held unusually upright and composed. This suggests it was not yet bound to the ground, without inventing limbs.
- **After the curse (3:14):** it drops onto its belly into the dust, and its colors dull.
- **Speech:** the mouth barely moves, with no human lip-sync. The voice is close-miked, soft, reasonable and intimate. **No exaggerated hissing, no demonic filter, no reverb.**
- **Acting:** patient stillness, small head tilts, watching Eve's eyes.
- **Forbidden:** red eyes, smoke, dark auras, fangs dripping venom, cartoon villainy. Genesis 3 calls it only "the serpent," and so does this production.

### CH-CHERUBIM & THE FLAMING SWORD (Genesis 3:24)
**Appears:** 10C
- **Placement:** "at the east of the garden of Eden."
- **Cherubim:** vast (three to four times human height) and always seen *partially*: the edge of a wing, the layered mass of a body, never a full clean "character shot." Wings are layered like hammered bronze and ash, not white dove feathers. Faces are obscured by wings or lost in the sword's glare. They barely move, and the stillness is what makes them frightening.
- **The flaming sword "which turned every way":** a blade or wheel of living fire rotating continually on shifting axes. **It is the real light source of the scene:** orange flicker on the ground, heat haze, sparks falling and dying.
- **Sound:** a low roar of flame and a deep rustle of wings.
- **Forbidden:** white robes, halos, pretty androgynous faces, armor, a sword held like a katana.

### Later characters
Noah and family, Abraham, Sarah, Hagar, and everyone else on the roster are **reserved**. Each will be designed at the start of their arc, with a turnaround sheet, expression sheet, costume states and aging sheet, and approved before first use.

## 9. VOICE: NARRATOR, GOD, CHARACTERS

**NARRATOR**
- One voice for all 148 episodes: mature (50s–60s), warm, low-mid register, unhurried.
- **Pace:** about 110–125 words per minute, with real pauses.
- Delivery changes with the story: awe (Creation), peace (Eden), dread (Fall), terror (Flood), mystery (Abraham), drama (Joseph). No trailer voice and no shouting Scripture.
- If an AI voice is used, lock one voice and its settings for the entire series.

**GOD**
- One distinct voice, locked across the series. Calm, quiet authority, spoken at conversational volume; **the world reacts, not the voice.**
- **No** pitch-shifting, robotic doubling, heavy cathedral reverb or bass boost. At most, a natural sense of vast space.

**KJV pronunciation standard**
- Pronounce "-eth" endings fully. "Subtil" = "subtle." Thee/thou read naturally, not archaically dramatized.
- Use traditional English pronunciations of names (for example, AY-bruh-ham), consistent across the series. Build a pronunciation list per arc.

**Narration density rule (v1.3)**
- Runtime is 61–90 seconds (§1.2). At series pace, an episode carries about **65–130 KJV words**, depending on how much silence the episode needs.
- Every episode's runtime is computed from its cue sheet (`tools/cues_arc01.py`) by `tools/tracks.py`, which fails any episode outside 61–90 seconds. **Re-time to the recorded narration before generating final motion clips.**
- Verse selection follows §13.

**Characters:** each voice is cast and locked on first appearance. Lip movement is subtle; characters speak while doing something (see the Quality Directive, §18).

## 10. SOUND & MUSIC

**Sound philosophy:** sound makes the world physical, and silence makes it holy. Before any major divine act, drop to near or total silence for 1.5–3 seconds.

**Instrument palette:** strings (especially low strings and solo cello), duduk- or ney-like reed, lyre or harp, frame drum and low hand percussion, wordless solo voice, sparing low brass.
**Forbidden:** synth risers, trailer "braams," constant epic choir, generic worship-pop.

## 11. SUBTITLES & TITLES

**Captions** are burned in (not TikTok auto-captions) for full control.
- **Font:** a semibold book serif for legibility on phones (for example, EB Garamond SemiBold or Crimson Pro SemiBold).
- **Color:** off-white (#F2EDE4) with a soft 30–40% dark drop shadow. No outline glow, no colored text.
- **Position:** about 62–72% of the way down the frame, above the TikTok caption area and clear of the right-hand buttons.
- **Line length:** at most 2 lines, about 28–32 characters per line. Break at KJV punctuation or natural clause breaks.
- **Speech:** no speaker labels and no quotation marks (the KJV has none). The picture and voice carry who is speaking.

## 12. HIGGSFIELD WORKFLOW & PROMPT STANDARD

**Workflow: keyframe first, then motion**
1. **Reference sheets first.** Before a character's first scene, generate and approve a turnaround (front, 3/4, profile) and an expression sheet for each costume state. Attach them as reference images every time that character is generated.
2. **Keyframe.** Generate the shot's opening frame as a still image. Make 3–6 variants, choose one, and run it through the Slop Test (§15).
3. **Motion.** Animate from the approved keyframe (image-to-video). If the model supports first-frame/last-frame control, use it for transformation shots such as darkness to light.
4. **Chaining.** For continuous action, use the approved last frame of shot N as the first frame of shot N+1.
5. **Camera presets.** If using a camera-motion preset, pick the one that matches the written move exactly. Reject any generation with unrequested camera motion.

**Prompt template**
```
[SHOT ID] — [DURATION]
KEYFRAME (image):
Vertical 9:16. [Subject, verbatim from Character Bible + costume state].
[Pose / action at this frame]. [Environment ID + specific details].
[Time; light SOURCE and direction]. Camera: [height, angle, lens mm, composition, depth].
Style: prestige hand-drawn TV anime, painterly background art, restrained natural color,
subtle film grain. Continuity: [what must match previous shots].
MOTION (video):
[Camera move + speed]. [Subject motion]. [Environment motion]. [Duration].
Keep still: [what must NOT move].
AVOID: [short, shot-specific list]
```

The positive description is always the main instruction. The AVOID list stays short and specific to the shot.

**Standard AVOID core** (trim per shot): generic AI aesthetic, plastic skin, waxy faces, malformed hands, morphing anatomy, floating objects, excessive glow, lens flare, god rays, oversaturation, static posing, weightless movement, videogame-cutscene look, modern objects, fantasy armor.

## 13. SCRIPTURE STANDARD

- **Text:** the standard KJV (Authorized Version) only. Words inside a quoted span are **never altered**.
- **Verse selection (approved v1.3):** each episode lists its full verse range and the exact verses narrated.
  - Omissions happen **only at whole-verse boundaries**. No mid-verse cuts or ellipses in narration.
  - **Never omitted:** divine speech; any character's dialogue; commands, blessings, curses, promises and covenants; genealogical facts that matter to the story; anything whose removal would change meaning.
  - **May be omitted:** narration that restates a divine command already spoken, in the form "God made…" (e.g. 1:17–18 after 1:14–15), and descriptive digressions whose content is shown on screen (e.g. the river names, 2:11–14).
  - **Split before omitting:** if an episode can reach 61–90 seconds by splitting it (§1.2), split it instead.
  - Every package prints the **full passage** with omitted verses marked, so nothing is lost from the long-form cut.
  - `tools/tracks.py` verifies that the narrated text equals the KJV verbatim (range minus the listed omissions) and that every quoted script line is KJV.
- **Arc I omissions (complete list):** 1:17–18 (Ep 03A), 1:25 (Ep 03B), 2:11–14 (Ep 06B), 2:19 (Ep 07A). All are narration only; no speech is omitted.
- **Adaptation:** any non-KJV spoken line is labeled **ADAPTATION — NOT KJV** in the script, used rarely, and never delivered as if it were Scripture. Default: prefer silence and acting over invented dialogue.
- **Sun rule:** see §4.4.

## 14. CONTINUITY SYSTEM

**IDs**
- Characters: `CH-ADAM`, with costume states `ADAM-A/B/C`
- Environments: `E-01` …
- Shots: `E01-S03` (episode 01, shot 03)
- Motifs: `M-01` …

**Keyframe library:** store every approved keyframe as `E01-S03_key.png` and the final clip as `E01-S03_v2.mp4`. The last approved frame of each episode is the continuity reference for the next.

## 15. PER-SHOT QA (THE GATE)

Every generated shot must pass all of these before it enters the edit:

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| Story | What does this shot communicate? |
| Character | Is anyone acting, not posing? |
| Camera | Does the position and move serve the moment? |
| Environment | Does the world feel physically real and inhabited? |
| Continuity | Does it match the Bible and the previous shot? |
| Physics | Do weight, contact and motion make sense? |
| Fidelity | Does it match the KJV text and the sun and God rules? |
| **Slop Test** | Would a viewer instantly recognize this as generic AI footage? **If yes, redesign the shot.** |
